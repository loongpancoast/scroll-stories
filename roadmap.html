A briefing for golf parents — three acts

For most families, recruiting starts with a question nobody can answer: is my kid actually good enough to play in college? Here is the honest frame — what coaches look at first, and why the highlight reel is the last thing that matters.

Act I — The invitation

Coaches recruit scorecards, not swings.

The first filter is brutally simple: can this kid shoot the number, on real courses, under pressure, more than once? Everything in the roadmap starts there — building a tournament schedule that produces a scorecard a coach can trust, instead of a swing video a coach will skim.

Act II — The standard

Recruiting has a clock. Most families start it late.

Coaches build their boards years out. The roadmap lays the contact timeline against your graduation year — when film goes out, when calls happen, when visits matter — so you are early instead of apologizing for being late.

Act III — The decision

Fit beats prestige. Every time.

The right program is the one where your game gets better and your degree means something. The roadmap ends with a fit filter — coaching style, playing opportunity, academics, cost — so the choice is a decision, not a gamble.
